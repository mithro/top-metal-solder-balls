# Sputtered or evaporated UBM

Vacuum-deposited under-bump metal (UBM) is the industrial standard. This page
covers how it would be applied to loose die, what it needs and how well it
fits.

## How it works

Under vacuum, sputter-etch the oxide off the pad, then deposit an
adhesion/barrier layer (Ti or TiW), a wettable layer (Ni, NiV or Cu) and a
thin Au cap (100 to 200 nm) ([ubm-interface]). Patterning is by
photolithography and etch or lift-off on wafers. For loose die the practical
options are a shadow mask aligned over the pads, or mounting die in a carrier
wafer and doing lift-off lithography.

## Equipment and vendors

A sputter or e-beam system with in-situ etch, plus a laser-cut shadow mask.
University cleanrooms and MEMS foundries have this equipment. At 200 µm-plus
features and 0.4 mm pitch a shadow mask is realistic, which is not true at
wire-bond pad sizes.

## Cost

Cleanroom hourly rates plus masks. A rough guess is a few hundred to low
thousands of dollars per run at an academic facility.

## Die versus wafer

Best on wafers. On die it needs a carrier, a mask and alignment to maybe
20 µm, done by hand under a microscope.

## Reliability

Excellent when the wettable layer is thick enough. A thin sputtered Ni (a few
hundred nm) can be consumed by SAC solder over multiple reflows, which is why
production flows plate thick Cu or Ni on top.

## Fit

**Medium.** Attractive if cleanroom access exists, and it avoids eating
the pad. Without in-situ oxide removal the contact resistance is unreliable.
