# sky130 comparison

SkyWater sky130 is the closest open comparison to GF180MCU: similar die size,
aluminium top metal, and a published ball grid layout for the Caravel chip.
This page gives the sky130 pad and bump geometry and what it implies for a
home method on GF180MCU.

sky130 never put a ball on aluminium. It added polymer, a copper
redistribution layer and under-bump metal first, at a packaging house. The
useful part is the geometry, which closely matches the
[target in this project](background.md).

This page is for comparison only. All tests in this project will be on
GF180MCU die.

## Probe pads on the open test tile

The [sky130 raw data repository][sky130-raw] holds transistor and ring
oscillator measurements taken on test tiles. It documents two tiles.

**The open test tile** ([sky130-open-tile]), built by Google, NIST and the
University of Michigan and first run on MPW-5, gives its pad geometry in a
drawing of the test interface:

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Probe pad size | 40 × 40 µm |
| Pad pitch along a row | 60 µm, so 20 µm between pads |
| Pad pitch between rows | 80 µm, so 40 µm between rows |
| Pads per group in the drawing | 6 columns × 3 rows |
| Device position | in the gap between rows, next to the pad it connects to |
| Pads on the tile | over 1400 |
| Contact method | probe card with needles |

```{figure} /_static/diagrams/sky130-probe-pads.svg
:alt: Grid of 40 micron probe pads at 60 micron pitch in rows 80 microns apart
:width: 100%

The probe pad grid of the open sky130 test tile, redrawn from the test
interface drawing in the repository.
```

**The SkyWater test tile** is documented only as a spreadsheet of what each
probe pad connects to, in modules of 12 pads. It gives no pad size or pitch.
It does list a continuity structure (a 10 µm metal line joining all pads) and
an alignment structure (metal lines 1.0 µm outside every pad), which are
useful ideas for a bump test die.

Neither tile states the pad metal or the passivation opening, and these are
probe pads, not bump pads. At 60 µm pitch they are too fine for solder
balls: one 250 µm ball would cover a block of about 4 × 3 pads. What they do
show is how small a pad can be when the contact is a needle, which matters
for the pressure contact idea in [Untried ideas](research.md). The bump
numbers below come from the sky130 PDK rules and the bump generator in
open_pdks.

## sky130 PDK rules that touch pads

From the [sky130 periphery rules][sky130-rules] and process assumptions:

| Item | sky130 | GF180MCU (wafer.space) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top metal | met5, aluminium, 1.2 µm or 2 µm thick depending on flow | Metal5, aluminium, 1.1 µm |
| Top metal minimum width and space | 1.6 µm | 0.44 µm and 0.46 µm |
| Redistribution layer in the PDK | yes, `rdl`, copper | no |
| Redistribution minimum width and space | 10 µm | not applicable |
| Redistribution enclosure of pad | 10.75 µm | not applicable |
| Redistribution to seal ring | 15 µm | not applicable |
| Polymer over pads | polyimide or PBO, rules reference an outside bumping house | none published |
| Foundry bump rules | none in the open rules, bumps are defined by the packaging house | section 9.4.3, rules TM.1 to TM.10 ([background](background.md)) |

The sky130 thickness values are the ones the PDK lists for antenna
calculations. No statement of the pad metal alloy was found for either
process.

## The Caravel ball grid, on both processes

[open_pdks] ships a bump bond generator written for the Micross
post-processing flow ([bump-gen]). Caravel has a script that uses it for
sky130 ([caravel-bump-sky130]) and a second one for GF180MCU
([caravel-bump-gf180]). The GF180MCU die in that script is 3.88 × 5.07 mm,
which is the wafer.space full slot.

```{figure} /_static/diagrams/caravel-bump-layout.svg
:width: 100%

The Caravel bump layout for GF180MCU, redrawn from the generator script. Bump
positions and pad pitches are from the script. How far the pads sit from the
die edge is not.
```

| Item | Value in the scripts |
| --- | --- |
| Bump array | 6 columns × 10 rows = 60 bumps |
| Bump pitch | 500 µm |
| Nominal bump diameter | 250 µm |
| Under-bump metal | 250 µm diameter (125 µm radius in the code) |
| Opening in the second polymer layer | 220 µm diameter |
| Copper pad under the bump | 270 µm diameter |
| Copper trace width | 15 µm default |
| Opening in the first polymer layer over a bond pad | pad opening shrunk by 10 µm per side |
| Copper overlap of that opening | 10 µm per side |
| Layers added on top of the foundry die | polymer 1, copper redistribution, polymer 2, under-bump metal |
| sky130 Caravel die | 3.588 × 5.188 mm |
| GF180MCU Caravel die | 3.88 × 5.07 mm |
| GF180MCU bond pad openings | 60 × 60 µm |
| GF180MCU bond pad pitch | 205 µm (left), 215 µm (right), 275 µm (top and bottom) |
| GF180MCU bond pads | 21 left, 20 right, 11 top, 11 bottom |

One comment in the generator says the under-bump metal is 15 µm smaller than
the bump, while the code draws it at the full 250 µm. The table follows the
code.

No record was found that the GF180MCU version was built. The script shows
that the layout was planned.

## What this means for a home method

- **The target geometry is proven.** 250 µm balls at 0.5 mm pitch on a die
  this size is what the proprietary Micross flow used. The 8 × 10 array in
  [background](background.md) is the same idea at a slightly tighter pitch.
- **The proprietary flow needed redistribution because the pads were small and
  at the edge.** A 60 µm wire bond pad cannot take a 250 µm ball. On
  wafer.space a custom pad layout is allowed, so large top metal pads can be
  drawn directly under each ball and the copper layer is not needed. FC01
  does this ([past runs](past-runs.md)).
- **Nobody skipped the under-bump metal.** Even with copper redistribution the
  flow ends in a separate solderable layer. That supports ranking nickel on
  the pad first among the home methods ([zincate and
  nickel](methods/zincate-nickel.md)).
- **A pad of about 220 to 250 µm under a 250 µm ball** is the proportion the
  proprietary flow uses. The GF180MCU bump rules allow it at 0.5 mm pitch
  ([background](background.md)).
- **sky130 top metal is barely thicker than GF180MCU top metal.** At 1.2 µm in
  the thinner flow, against 1.1 µm, it gives almost no extra margin for
  zincate, which is one reason the
  proprietary flow does not plate on it directly.
