# Stud bumping, then solder

A wire bonder can leave a gold stud on each pad for solder to wet. This page
covers how stud bumping works, who offers it for single die, and why it is
unreliable under solder.

## How it works

A ball bonder makes a normal thermosonic ball bond on the Al pad and then
breaks the wire off, leaving a gold (or copper) stud about 60 to 120 µm across[^hybrid]. The ultrasonic scrub breaks the oxide, so no chemistry or mask is
needed. Studs can be coined flat. The stud is then either used as the bump
itself (thermocompression, or dipped in
[conductive adhesive](../methods/adhesives.md)) or used as a pedestal that
solder wets.

## Equipment and vendors

A manual ball bonder such as a K&S 4524 handles 18 to 75 µm gold wire[^ks4524]; used units are sold by equipment brokers, prices on request.

Services that advertise single-die stud bumping:

- Quik-Pak / QP Technologies ("individual die can be directly bumped without
  requiring under bump metallization", [^quikpak]).
- ALTER Technology[^alter].
- Hybrid SA[^hybrid].
- CMC Microsystems routes Canadian users to QP Technologies and 3IT.Micro[^cmc].

## Cost

Stud bumping is serial, so cost scales with bump count. 20 to 110 bumps per
die is a small job.

## Die versus wafer

Naturally die-level. Any bonder with a die chuck works.

## Reliability

Reliability is the weak point when the stud is used under a solder ball.

- Au on Al forms intermetallics on heating. Sandia found joints made with
  studs directly on Al pads unreliable once processed around 200 °C and above,
  and plated Ni/Au on the pads first[^sandia]. SAC reflow peaks near 245 °C.
- A gold stud is "an infinite source of gold" for tin. AuSn₄ embrittles the
  joint; one article describes poor yield from studs on pre-tinned pads until a
  UBM was added over the stud[^bump-buck]. Guidance for ordinary joints is to
  keep gold below about 3 wt%[^calpoly-au].
- Copper studs avoid gold embrittlement but need more ultrasonic force, which
  risks cracking under the pad, need forming gas, are less widely offered, and
  Cu-Al intermetallic data is thin[^bump-buck][^cu-stud].
- A stud footprint of about 80 µm is small for a 250 µm ball, so the ball's
  strength would rest on a narrow neck unless several studs are clustered per
  pad or the pad is otherwise sealed.

## Variants

PacTech's laser jetting has been used with gold studs as the UBM for single
chips (60 µm SAC balls for Belle II prototypes)[^desy]; the laser reflow is
milliseconds long, which limits both gold dissolution and Au-Al growth.
Low-temperature solder (Sn-Bi, about 140 °C liquidus) or In-based solder would
also keep the Au-Al interface cooler.

## Fit

**Medium.** Fast and chemistry-free for proving electrical function.
Not a route to parts that survive repeated reflow or thermal cycling.
