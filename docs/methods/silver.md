# Silver mirror and other silver deposits

This method uses the school silver mirror reaction to put a solderable silver
film on the pads. Tollens' reagent (silver nitrate, ammonia, sodium hydroxide)
plus glucose deposits bright silver on whatever it touches. The problems below
make it a poor fit for solder, and the page ends with the uses silver may
still have.

## Known problems and unknowns

1. **It coats everything.** Silver deposits on the passivation as readily as
   on the pads, so all pads end up shorted. The pads have to be masked before
   and the mask lifted after, or the silver removed between pads.
2. **It does not remove the oxide.** The silver sits on top of the aluminium
   oxide, so the contact to the pad underneath may be poor even though the
   surface looks good (unsourced).
3. **The bath attacks aluminium.** Tollens' reagent is strongly alkaline, and
   aluminium dissolves in alkali (unsourced; no source on Tollens' reagent
   with thin aluminium films was found).
4. **Silver dissolves in solder.** Thin silver vanishes into molten tin almost
   at once, a known problem called leaching. Advice for thin silver
   terminations is short reflow, silver-bearing solder, or indium solder,
   which dissolves silver far more slowly ([ag-leaching], [ag-dissolution]).
   A 0.1 µm mirror would be gone in well under a second, leaving solder on
   bare aluminium (unsourced).
5. **Silver on aluminium corrodes.** Mirror makers put a barrier between the
   two metals to stop galvanic attack ([ag-mirror-al-patent]).

## Safety

Tollens' reagent must be made fresh and never stored. On standing it forms
silver nitride, a shock-sensitive explosive, and it has injured students
([tollens], [rsc-mirror]). Destroy leftovers with dilute acid.

## Where silver could still help

- **As a surface for [silver epoxy](adhesives.md).** No molten solder, so no
  leaching. A silver film deposited right after an oxide-removing dip would
  give the epoxy a noble surface to grip.
- **Displacement silver, which is selective.** Aluminium is reactive enough to
  plate silver out of a silver salt solution by itself, only where there is
  bare metal. One study found displacement works on aluminium films alloyed
  with about 0.5% copper but not on pure aluminium ([brevnov]). Chip top metal
  is often an aluminium-copper alloy, though the alloy is not confirmed for
  GF180MCU ([open questions](../next-steps.md#open-questions)). Displacement
  silver has a reputation for poor adhesion ([immersion-ag-patent]). See
  [untried ideas](../research.md).
- **Silver over zincate.** The aerospace recipe for solderable aluminium is
  [zincate](zincate-nickel.md), then copper, then tin ([ams2420]). Zincate
  then silver is an untested home variation.

## Fit

**Low for solder, medium as a helper for epoxy.** The chemistry is the easiest
here, but silver gives a noble top surface and does nothing about the oxide
underneath.
